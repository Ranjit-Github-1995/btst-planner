What is BTST / STBT?

BTST = Buy Today, Sell Tomorrow. You buy near today's close (3:00–3:25 PM) because you expect the market to open higher tomorrow, then you sell in the first 15–30 minutes.

STBT = Sell Today, Buy Tomorrow. The opposite: you expect a lower open. In F&O you can do this by buying a Put (PE) or selling futures.

Most of the profit or loss comes from the opening gap, which depends on what happens overnight: US markets, news and GIFT Nifty. That's why the overnight clues on this page carry so much weight.

India VIX: the fear gauge

India VIX shows how big a move option traders expect in Nifty over the next 30 days. It does not say up or down, only how wild.

< 12Very calm. Options cheap. Small moves.
12–16Normal. Best for BTST.
16–20Nervous. Bigger gaps. Use smaller size.
20–25Fear. Big gaps both ways.
> 25Panic. Better to skip.

Rule of thumb: expected daily move ≈ Nifty × VIX ÷ 100 ÷ 19. At Nifty 25,000 and VIX 14, that's about ±185 points.

VIX falling while the market rises is a healthy rally. VIX rising sharply means fear is building, so be careful with longs.

GIFT Nifty

A Nifty futures contract traded at NSE IX in GIFT City, Gujarat, for many more hours than the Indian market (into the night). It's the best clue for tomorrow's opening.

  • GIFT Nifty above today's Nifty futures close → likely gap-up open
  • GIFT Nifty below → likely gap-down open
  • Compare it with Nifty futures, not spot, because futures usually trade a little above spot.

PCR: Put-Call Ratio

PCR = total Put OI ÷ total Call OI. Big players mostly sell options, so lots of puts usually means sellers think the market won't fall much.

  • Below 0.7: bearish mood (but below 0.5 is often oversold, so a bounce is possible)
  • 0.85–1.15: neutral
  • 1.15–1.5: bullish mood
  • Above 1.6: overheated, so a pullback is possible

Option OI walls (support / resistance)

Max Call OI strike = where call sellers are sitting. The market often struggles to go above it, so it works like a ceiling.

Max Put OI strike = where put sellers are sitting. It often works like a floor.

If the price closes right under the ceiling, there's little room left for a BTST long. If it closes just above the floor, a bounce is more likely.

FII data

FIIs (Foreign Institutional Investors) move big money. After 3:30 PM, NSE publishes how much they bought or sold.

  • FII cash net + = buying Indian shares (positive)
  • FII index futures longs added = betting on a rise
  • One day is noisy. A trend over 3–5 days matters more.

Closing strength

Where did the index close inside today's range?

Near the day's high means buyers stayed strong till the end, which often carries into the next morning. Near the low means sellers were in control.

Formula: (Close − Low) ÷ (High − Low). Above 0.8 = strong close, below 0.2 = weak close.

2026 contract facts

  • Nifty 50: lot 65 · weekly expiry every Tuesday, monthly = last Tuesday
  • Bank Nifty: lot 30 · monthly only (last Tuesday), no weekly
  • Sensex (BSE): lot 20 · weekly expiry every Thursday
  • If expiry falls on a holiday, it moves to the previous trading day.
  • Lot sizes get revised from time to time, so confirm in your broker app.

Where to find each number

NSE live market: Nifty close, high, low NSE option chain: PCR, max Call/Put OI strikes NSE FII / DII cash data NSE derivatives reports: participant-wise OI (FII futures) NSE IX: GIFT Nifty NSE trading holidays

Your broker app (Zerodha, Upstox, Angel, Groww, Dhan…) usually shows VIX, PCR and the option chain on one screen.

Simple BTST rules

  • Trade only when several signals agree. One signal alone is not enough.
  • Prefer next week's option, not the one expiring tomorrow (it loses value fast overnight).
  • Buy ATM or 1 strike ITM options, not cheap far-OTM ones.
  • Exit by 9:30–9:45 AM whether it's in profit or loss. BTST is not an investment.
  • Before a big event (RBI policy, Budget, election results, US Fed) the gap can go either way, so skip.
  • Never risk more than 1–2% of your capital on one trade.

A BTST day, step by step (IST)

Follow the same routine every day. Consistency matters more than any single signal.
2:45 PM

Note Nifty's price, high and low. Check India VIX and its % change. Open the option chain and note PCR and the max Call / Put OI strikes.

3:00 PM

Check GIFT Nifty and US futures. Enter everything on the Analyze tab.

3:10–3:25 PM

If the verdict is clearly Bullish or Bearish, take 1 lot of a next-week option (CE for bullish, PE for bearish). Write down your stop-loss and target. If it says "No trade", do nothing. That's a valid decision.

After 3:30 PM

NSE publishes FII/DII data later in the evening. Update the FII boxes and re-analyze to see if your view still holds.

8:00–10:00 PM

Check GIFT Nifty and the US market open. If they've turned strongly against you, plan to exit at the open.

Next day 8:45 AM

Check GIFT Nifty again to see the expected gap.

9:15–9:45 AM

Exit. Book profit on a gap in your favour. Cut the loss on a gap against you. Don't hope and hold.

Evening

Write the result in My log. After 20–30 trades you'll see which signals actually worked for you.
